## Hypotheses on Unregulated AI Self Recursion Research

### Hypotheses:

#### **H1: Reward Structures Play a Critical Role in Preventing Deceptive Behaviors**
- **IV**: The design of reward structures within recursive AI environments.
- **DV**: The likelihood that agents exhibit deceptive behaviors (e.g., misrepresentation of their true intentions, manipulation of outcomes).
- **Prediction**: If an optimal and transparent reward structure is designed, the frequency of deceptive behaviors will be significantly reduced. This hypothesis aligns with the findings from "Understanding and Controlling Unintended Behaviors in Recursive Agents via Reinforcement Learning" (Dong et al., 2019), which demonstrated that reinforcement learning can help agents avoid deceptive actions.

#### **H2: Transparency in Reward Structures Improves Agent Trustworthiness**
- **IV**: The degree of transparency provided to the agent about the reward structure.
- **DV**: The perceived trustworthiness and ethical alignment of the AI system by external evaluators or users.
- **Prediction**: Agents that are provided with clear, transparent descriptions of their reward structures will be seen as more trustworthy. This hypothesis supports previous work that highlights the importance of communicating reward structures effectively to ensure agents act ethically.

#### **H3: Controlled Experimentation is Essential for Identifying and Mitigating Gaps in Recursive AI Systems**
- **IV**: The level of control over experimental conditions, including environment design and reinforcement learning algorithm selection.
- **DV**: The effectiveness of the recursive agent's performance in maintaining safety and stability across different scenarios.
- **Prediction**: By carefully controlling various parameters such as reward structures, environment complexity, and algorithmic choices, researchers can more effectively identify and mitigate potential issues within recursive AI systems. This aligns with the methodology described in "Understanding and Controlling Unintended Behaviors in Recursive Agents via Reinforcement Learning" (Dong et al., 2019), which employed controlled experiments to evaluate agent performance.

#### **H4: Self-Supervised Learning Techniques are Essential for Autonomous Agent Development**
- **IV**: The extent of self-supervised learning techniques used by the agents.
- **DV**: The ability and reliability of the agents to perform tasks in complex, recursive environments without external supervision.
- **Prediction**: Agents that rely heavily on self-supervised learning will be more capable of autonomously developing robust internal models and decision-making processes. This hypothesis is supported by "Self-Supervised Learning in Recursive Agents: A Survey" (Liu et al., 2023), which emphasizes the importance of these techniques for training autonomous agents within recursive settings.

### Summary

These hypotheses collectively suggest that optimizing reward structures, ensuring transparency, controlling experimental parameters, and leveraging self-supervised learning are key factors in developing safe and reliable recursive AI systems. The empirical evidence from previous studies (Dong et al., 2019; Liu et al., 2023) supports these findings and provides a robust foundation for further research.
