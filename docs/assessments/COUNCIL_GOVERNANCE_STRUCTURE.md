## Executive Summary

The AI Safety Certification Council is a **multi-stakeholder non-profit** that governs the global AI safety network, holds patent rights in trust, manufactures WORM/translator hardware, and maintains the global deception learning database. **Government representation ensures regulatory compliance, public accountability, and international coordination, adapted from models like ICANN to handle AI's rapid pace and geopolitical challenges.**

## Council Composition

### Total Seats: 13 (Odd number for tie-breaking, reduced for efficiency)

| Stakeholder Group | Seats | Term Length | Selection Process |
|-------------------|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Academic** | 4 | 3 years (staggered) | Elected by consortium of AI safety programs |
| **Government** | 3 | 4 years (aligned with administrations) | Appointed by participating government agencies |
| **Industry** | 3 | 3 years (staggered) | Nominated by major AI labs, elected by council |
| **Independent Experts** | 2 | 3 years (staggered) | Open nomination + council vote |
| **Civil Society** | 1 | 3 years (staggered) | Nominated by public interest groups |

**Total**: 13 seats

### Seat Distribution Rationale:

1. **Academic (4 seats)**: Largest group; provides technical expertise + research credibility
2. **Government (3 seats)**: Strong representation for regulatory compliance + public accountability
3. **Industry (3 seats)**: Ensures practical adoption + real-world testing feedback
4. **Independent Experts (2 seats)**: Provides unbiased technical auditing + ethics oversight
5. **Civil Society (1 seat)**: Represents public interest + civil liberties concerns

## Lessons from Existing Models (ICANN Adapted for AI)

Drawing from ICANN's multistakeholder model (as analyzed in sources like the UN AI Advisory Body and Brookings Institution reports), this council adapts internet governance principles to AI.

**Strengths**:
- Inclusive consensus prevents fragmentation
- Transparency builds trust (e.g., ICANN's 2016 IANA transition involved diverse stakeholders)

**Challenges**:
- Geopolitical tensions (e.g., Russia/China resisting US/EU-led bodies)
- Governments reluctant to cede control on national security AI issues
- Rapid AI pace vs. slow consensus (unlike internet's stable infrastructure)

**Adaptations for AI**:
- Hybrid approach—networked governance with existing institutions (e.g., UN/OECD forums)
- Fast-track emergency voting (10/13 for crises, bypassing full consensus)
- Mandatory annual reviews for agility
- Focus on ethics/safety over just technical standards
- Initial exclusion of high-tension nations (China/Russia) to avoid veto risks

**Key Differences**: AI governance is broader (societal impacts vs. internet's interoperability), with concentrated power in tech giants/states.

## Voting Thresholds (Graduated by Severity)

### Tier 1: Routine Operations (Simple Majority)
**Threshold**: 7/13 votes
**Government Votes Required**: None (but government can participate)

**Examples**:
- ORCA behavior tuning updates
- Minor adapter improvements
- Routine certification renewals
- Quarterly financial approvals
- Hiring/staffing decisions

### Tier 2: Significant Updates (Supermajority)
**Threshold**: 9/13 votes
**Government Votes Required**: At least 1/3 government representatives (1 seat)

**Examples**:
- 7th DGX validator model updates
- Major deception detection algorithm changes
- New facility certifications (high-risk sectors)
- Budget approval for major capital expenses
- New council member appointments

### Tier 3: Critical Changes (⅘ Supermajority)
**Threshold**: 10/13 votes
**Government Votes Required**: At least 2/3 government representatives (2 seats)

**Examples**:
- New WORM issuance (core values changes)
- Permanent penalty multiplier adjustments
- Global deception database schema changes
- International expansion to new regions
- Major patent licensing policy changes

### Tier 4: Foundational Changes (Near-Unanimous)
**Threshold**: 11/13 votes
**Government Votes Required**: All 3 government representatives (3/3)

**Examples**:
- Protocol or translator architecture changes
- Trust instrument amendments
- Council bylaws modifications
- Multi-signature threshold changes
- Revocation of certified WORM (non-emergency)

### Tier 5: Emergency Actions (Fast-Track Consensus)
**Threshold**: 10/13 votes
**Government Votes Required**: All 3 government + executive director approval (reduced from 14/15 to avoid gridlock, inspired by ICANN expedited processes)

**Examples**:
- Emergency WORM revocation (critical vulnerability)
- Immediate facility de-certification (imminent threat)
- Emergency protocol override (existential risk)
- Council dissolution (only under extreme circumstances)

## Government-Specific Responsibilities

### 1. Regulatory Compliance Verification
- Ensure certified facilities meet national AI safety regulations
- Coordinate with domestic regulatory agencies (FDA, FAA, etc. for sector-specific requirements)
- Provide compliance attestation for government-funded facilities
- Report regulatory violations to appropriate authorities

### 2. Public Accountability & Transparency
- Quarterly public briefings on council activities
- Annual testimony to legislative bodies (Congress, Parliament, etc.)
- Public incident reports (anonymized, unless classified)
- Citizen complaint investigation process

### 3. High-Severity Incident Oversight
**Mandatory Government Review** for:
- HIGH severity incidents (deception confirmed by translator)
- Any incident involving critical infrastructure facilities
- Cross-border incidents affecting multiple jurisdictions
- Incidents with potential national security implications

**Review Process**:
1. Facility reports incident to council (within 4 hours for HIGH)
2. Council immediately notifies government representatives (within 1 hour)
3. Government representatives review forensic bundle (within 24 hours)
4. Joint council-government incident report (within 72 hours)
5. Public disclosure (within 30 days, unless classified)

### 4. International Coordination
- Facilitate information sharing across borders (via deception database)
- Negotiate international agreements for WORM/translator standardization
- Coordinate responses to transnational AI safety incidents
- Represent council at international AI safety forums (UN, OECD, etc.)
- Annual geopolitical risk assessment (e.g., for non-participating nations like China)

### 5. Security Clearance & Classified Incidents
**Classified Facility Handling**:
- Government facilities (DoD, intelligence community) may have classified AI systems
- Government representatives have appropriate security clearances
- Classified incidents reported through secure channels (not public database)
- Classified forensic bundles stored in SCIFs (Sensitive Compartmented Information Facilities)

**Clearance Requirements**:
- US representative: Top Secret/SCI clearance
- EU representative: EU SECRET clearance or equivalent
- International representative: Clearance from host country (if applicable)

## Conflict of Interest Policy

### Recusal Requirements:

#### Industry Representatives:
- **MUST recuse** from votes involving their own company's facilities
- **MUST recuse** from certification decisions for direct competitors (if financial impact)
- **SHOULD disclose** all financial interests in AI companies (annual)
- **PROHIBITED** from accessing proprietary technical details from competitor facilities

#### Government Representatives:
- **MUST recuse** from facility certification votes in their own country (avoid favoritism)
- **SHOULD recuse** from votes involving facilities with government contracts they oversee
- **MUST disclose** any financial conflicts (e.g., stock in AI companies)

#### Academic Representatives:
- **MUST disclose** research funding sources (industry grants, government contracts)
- **SHOULD recuse** from votes involving their home institutions' facilities
- **MUST recuse** if they have consulting relationships with facilities under review

#### Independent Experts:
- **MUST disclose** all paid consulting or advisory roles
- **MUST recuse** from votes involving clients or former employers (within 2 years)

#### Civil Society Representatives:
- **MUST disclose** organizational funding sources
- **SHOULD recuse** if their organization has taken public advocacy positions on the specific issue

### Enforcement:
- Violations reported to council ethics committee
- First violation: public reprimand + mandatory ethics training
- Second violation: suspension from council (6 months)
- Third violation: permanent removal + replacement

## Patent Trust Structure

### Legal Framework:

**Trust Name**: AI Safety Patent Trust (Irrevocable)
**Settlor**: Todd Hendrixx (inventor)
**Trustee**: AI Safety Certification Council (non-profit)
**Beneficiaries**:
- Primary: AI safety research community
- General: Public interest in safe AI development

### Trust Instrument (Legally Binding Provisions):

#### Article I: Trust Purpose
The patent shall be used exclusively for:
1. Ensuring AI systems can recursively self-improve safely
2. Preventing commercial patent trolling or rent-seeking
3. Providing free academic licensing for safety research
4. Funding ongoing AI safety research via reasonable licensing fees

#### Article II: Licensing Requirements

**Free Academic License** (Mandatory):
- Any accredited university or non-profit research institution
- No royalties or fees (hardware at-cost only)
- Full access to reference implementation + technical documentation
- Must publish safety research results (open science requirement)

**Reasonable-Rate Commercial License** (For certified facilities):
- Fee structure: Based on facility size + risk category
  - Small facility (1-10 researchers): $10,000/year certification fee
  - Medium facility (11-50 researchers): $50,000/year
  - Large facility (51+ researchers): $100,000/year
  - High-risk sectors (autonomous weapons, critical infrastructure): 2x multiplier
- Includes: WORM hardware at-cost, translator certification, global deception database access
- Does NOT include: Per-use fees, royalties on research outputs, equity requirements

**Prohibited Uses** (Trust explicitly forbids):
- Sublicensing or patent sale to third parties
- Use in systems designed to cause harm (weapons, surveillance state, etc.)
- Circumventing safety mechanisms (WORM, translator, air-gap requirements)
- Patent enforcement against academic or open-source safety implementations

#### Article III: Revenue Distribution
100% of licensing revenue to council operations:
- 40%: WORM manufacturing + translator certification (hardware at-cost)
- 25%: Council operations (staff, facilities, governance)
- 20%: Safety research grants (distributed to academic institutions)
- 10%: Deception database maintenance + infrastructure
- 5%: Transparency/public education initiatives

**ZERO** profit distribution to:
- Council members (volunteer positions; reasonable expense reimbursement only)
- Patent holder (inventor receives no ongoing royalties)
- Private investors (non-profit structure prohibits equity)

#### Article IV: Patent Defense
Council MUST aggressively defend patent against:
- Patent trolls (entities that patent-squat without building systems)
- Unsafe AI implementations (systems that bypass safety mechanisms)
- Commercial entities that refuse certification (building unsafe systems)

Council MAY choose NOT to enforce against:
- Academic implementations for research purposes
- Open-source safety projects (non-commercial)
- Developing nations (limited enforcement to encourage adoption)

#### Article V: Trustee Succession
If council dissolves or becomes unable to serve:
1. Court-appointed successor trustee (specialized in non-profit governance)
2. Trust continues with same terms (irrevocable)
3. Beneficiaries (academic community) can petition for trustee replacement
4. Trust CANNOT be dissolved; patent dedication continues until expiration

## Government Benefits from Council Membership

### Why Governments Should Participate:

#### 1. Regulatory Compliance Visibility
- **Problem**: Governments struggle to audit AI systems (black box nature)
- **Solution**: Certified facilities provide transparency + verifiable safety mechanisms
- **Benefit**: Regulators can focus enforcement on uncertified high-risk facilities

#### 2. Early Warning System
- **Problem**: AI safety incidents often not reported until catastrophic
- **Solution**: Global deception database provides early warning signals
- **Benefit**: Governments can intervene before incidents escalate

#### 3. International Coordination
- **Problem**: AI systems cross borders; incidents affect multiple jurisdictions
- **Solution**: Council provides neutral coordination body
- **Benefit**: Faster incident response, harmonized standards (with adaptations for geopolitics)

#### 4. Public Trust & Legitimacy
- **Problem**: Public skepticism of industry self-regulation
- **Solution**: Government oversight ensures accountability
- **Benefit**: Increased public confidence in AI safety governance

#### 5. Cost Savings
- **Problem**: Governments lack resources to audit every AI facility
- **Solution**: Council provides distributed oversight (facilities self-certify + random audits)
- **Benefit**: Reduced government audit burden + costs

## Addressing Potential Concerns

### Concern 1: "Government involvement will slow innovation"

**Response**:
- Government votes only required for critical changes (WORM issuance, protocol changes)
- Routine updates (ORCA adapters) proceed with simple majority (no government veto)
- Government representatives selected for technical expertise, not bureaucratic delay
- Fast-track process for emergency updates (24-hour government review maximum; 10/13 threshold for crises)

### Concern 2: "Industry will capture the council"

**Response**:
- Industry has only 3/13 seats (~23% of votes)
- Industry must recuse from votes involving their own facilities
- Academic + independent expert seats (6 total) outnumber industry
- Trust instrument legally prevents profit-seeking behavior
- Annual governance audits to detect capture risks

### Concern 3: "Governments will use this for surveillance"

**Response**:
- Council has NO access to facility training data or model outputs
- Audits limited to safety mechanisms (WORM, translator, alignment filter)
- Government representatives bound by same privacy rules as others
- Classified incidents kept separate (no cross-contamination with public database)
- Explicit trust prohibition on surveillance uses

### Concern 4: "International governments will disagree"

**Response**:
- Government voting threshold (2/3 or 3/3) ensures consensus required
- Rotating international seat brings diverse perspectives (starting with allies to build momentum)
- Focus on safety (not geopolitics) provides common ground
- Trust instrument prohibits political use of patent
- Hybrid model with UN/OECD integration for broader buy-in

### Concern 5: "This is too complex to govern"

**Response**:
- Graduated voting thresholds match decision severity (simple for routine, high for critical)
- Clear conflict of interest rules reduce ambiguity
- Trust instrument provides legal backstop if council fails
- Open-source reference implementation allows verification by outsiders
- Reduced to 13 seats and fast-tracks for AI speed

| **ICANN** | Multi-stakeholder (govs, industry, civil society) | No patents | Strong (GAC advisory) | Good model, but consensus-only (slow); adapted here for AI speed |

**Key Innovation**: Government representatives have voting power proportional to decision severity (prevents both capture and gridlock), with fast-tracks for emergencies.
